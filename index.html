Case studies

Five projects, told straight

Ridecell first, then Prezent AI. Each one ends with what I would change.

People Operations built from zero

Ridecell | Dec 2018 - Jan 2026

Challenge

There was no documentation beyond outdated files, headcount lived in spreadsheets, and employees had no tracked channel for their questions.

What I did

Built a 20+ procedure SOP library with named owners and backup coverage, launched ASKPeople (Jira and Confluence) with tiered SLAs, and set up Sapling as the HR single source of truth, integrated with ADP and connected through Zapier.

Result

Supported growth to 5 countries, passed 6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits, cut approval cycles from 1-2 days to 2-3 hours, and resolved 5-10 tickets a day within 24-48 hours.

What I would do differently

I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

One approval path for every HRIS change

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

HRIS changes ran through an undocumented, unapproved process, which created audit risk. Some people pushed back early that a form would be too process-heavy.

What I did

Built a Claude-powered Employee Action Form that requires documentation and approval before any HRIS change, plus New Hire and Offboarding workflows in Notion.

Result

100% adoption within one to two months, an audit trail that had not existed, and preboarding within 24 hours of offer acceptance.

What I would do differently

I'd have piloted the form with one team first. Some people said it was too process-heavy, and early proof from a small group might have won them over faster.

A reorganization with cost visibility

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

A reorganization affecting 18 employees across the US and India needed accurate severance and COBRA numbers, and leaders had no view of what it would cost.

What I did

Built AI-enabled severance and cost modeling with Claude and Gemini that calculated severance, COBRA cost and COBRA months owed and drafted the agreements, tracked on a shared spreadsheet for Payroll and IT.

Result

First-time cost visibility on a reduction in force, and agreement prep cut from 20 minutes each to about 3 hours total.

What I would do differently

I'd have built the cost model before the reorganization was decided. Leaders could then have compared scenarios rather than just seeing the cost of the chosen one.

How I would start

A proposed plan for the first six months, built around the role description.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and map

  • Meet the People team, HR partners and the leaders who rely on them, and learn what works and where people wait.
  • Map the employee lifecycle, the systems, and the handoffs to Payroll, IT and Finance across regions.
  • Review current requests, service levels and escalation paths to see where work gets stuck.
Days 31 to 60

Design the first moves

  • Draft the People Operations operating model: who owns what, response times, escalation and backup coverage.
  • Run a focused audit of data and controls, the way I ran a 60-day compliance audit at Prezent AI, and flag the gaps.
  • Pick two or three quick wins, such as one approval path for every HRIS change.
Days 61 to 90

Ship and measure

  • Launch the first standard operating procedures and a simple case queue with agreed response times.
  • Set up a light dashboard so the team can see volume, response time and root causes.
  • Review what changed with the team and adjust.
By month 6

The outcome

  • A documented operating model the team owns, with named owners and backups.
  • Clean, governed people data and audit-ready documentation.
  • Routine requests handled through self-service and automation, so the team has more time for sensitive, human conversations.

This is a starting point. I would adjust it after the first conversations with the people I would be supporting and the leaders I would be working with.
